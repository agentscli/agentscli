The [modes chapter](/course/copilot/modes/) taught the judgment: match latitude to what a wrong answer costs. Roles put that judgment in the chat; **permissions** put it in a setting - the course's control principle made concrete. The role decides *how* Copilot works; the permission level decides *how much it may run without asking you first*.

Take the scenario this course follows: the change to `shared-lib` is ready - the library a dozen services import. Every consequential action during that change should cross your desk before it executes. One repo over, in your own `orders-service`, the same engineer wants almost nothing interrupted. Permissions are how both of those postures become deliberate settings instead of defaults you forgot you had.

## The lessons

- [Levels · manual, assisted, allow all](/course/copilot/permissions/levels/) - Manual permissions, Assisted permissions, and Allow all, plus where Autopilot actually fits.
- [The checkpoint · risky commands](/course/copilot/permissions/the-checkpoint/) - the approval prompt: the one place autonomy pauses at the default, and why on shared code you keep it.
- [Match · autonomy to blast radius](/course/copilot/permissions/match-to-stakes/) - the payoff: tight on the library, loose on your own app, same engineer, opposite settings.
