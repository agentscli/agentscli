A [prompt file](/course/copilot/prompt-files/) captures a procedure. But some work needs a *persona*: a reviewer that holds a library's compatibility rules, an investigator that only reads, a coordinator that delegates to other agents. Re-explaining "here is how this library treats breaking changes" at the top of every session is its own kind of repetition - the persona version of re-typing your conventions.

That is what a **custom agent** is: a named, reusable role you define yourself, saved as a file in your repo, bundling a job description, a scoped set of tools, and conventions into one thing you summon by name. And because a custom agent can be allowed to call *other* custom agents, you can build small teams - one coordinating agent fanning checks out to focused subagents.

This chapter builds the team you need for the riskiest shape of work in this course: a change to a shared library that other services import. In the running scenario that is `shared-lib`, imported by roughly a dozen services - a wrong move there does not break your app, it breaks apps you have never opened. That risk profile is exactly what justifies setting up real machinery before touching a line.

## Custom agents are not the built-in roles

Copilot's built-ins are Ask, Plan, and Agent in the [agents dropdown](/course/copilot/modes/) - the role picker in the Chat view. A custom agent is an entry *you* add to that same dropdown. The difference that matters: a built-in role is *how much freedom* Copilot has right now; a custom agent is *who Copilot is being* - a reviewer that knows the compatibility rules, an investigator that cannot edit, a coordinator that delegates. For high-consequence work where the rules are the whole point, that is not a convenience. It is the difference between a reviewer who knows the codebase and a stranger you brief from scratch every morning.
