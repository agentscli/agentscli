Every turn of agent work passes through three dials: the agent's *posture* (how much latitude it has before you look), its *context* (what files and instructions sit in front of it), and its *engine* (which model does the reasoning at all). This chapter is about the engine - the dial with a meter attached. Changing the agent's posture - its mode - is free, and so is pointing at the right file; reaching for the biggest model on a one-line fix is not.

Here's the trap. People find a model they trust, pin it, and pay for it on every prompt - burning a frontier model's tokens to stamp out a CRUD endpoint that any cheap model would have nailed. Or they leave it on the cheapest thing and watch it flail at a genuine reasoning problem that needed the heavy engine. Both are the same error: a fixed setting against a variable workload. The operator's move is to let the cheap, routed default carry the everyday work, and spend the expensive engine only on the turns that actually change the outcome.

Price the mistake before Cursor's own vocabulary comes in. What makes Cursor different from the single-vendor agents - and the reason this chapter exists at all - is that "the model" isn't one company's model. Cursor lets you pick across vendors per chat, route automatically when you don't care, and run its own in-house agentic model. Underneath all of it sits a plan-dependent billing structure (laid out in [Pricing, Composer, and context limits](/course/cursor/models/pricing-pools/)) that decides what a given request actually costs. Get the decision pattern straight and the rest of the chapter is just judgment.

## The moves of this chapter

- **The per-chat picker and Auto** - the picker is per-conversation, and Auto routes everyday work across models (as of September 2026; [models doc](https://cursor.com/docs/models)) without requiring you to pin anything - [The per-chat model picker and Auto](/course/cursor/models/picker-and-auto/)
- **Composer and billing** - Cursor maker Anysphere's own agentic model, and how your plan, your routing, and the model's context limits decide what a request costs - [Pricing, Composer, and context limits](/course/cursor/models/pricing-pools/)
- **Mixing models** - plan with a stronger model, build with a lower-cost one, and spend deliberately on the single turn that earned it - [Mixing models: plan with one, build with another](/course/cursor/models/mixing-models/)

The arc runs from the dial to the meter and back to judgment. The first lesson gives you the control surface and the default that should carry most of your week. The second names the in-house model worth understanding and shows the billing structure underneath - because once you can see how routing and usage interact, "which model?" stops being a taste question and becomes a spending one. The third puts both dials in the same loop: a strong engine on the design, an efficient one on the typing. The person who's merely *installed* Cursor pins a favorite model and pays for capability it never uses. By the end of this chapter you'll be the other kind of user - the one who lets Auto carry the everyday and reaches for stronger reasoning on purpose.
