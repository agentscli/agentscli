The last two chapters were about *posture* - how much latitude a task deserves before you look - and *material*: what the agent has in front of it when it answers. This one is about *engine*: which model answers at all, and what that choice costs. They're different dials, and the mistake is to wire them together in your head. A mode change is free, and so is pointing at the right file; reaching for the biggest model on a one-line fix is not.

Here's the trap. People find a model they trust, pin it, and pay for it on every prompt - burning a frontier model's tokens to stamp out a CRUD endpoint that any cheap model would have nailed. Or they leave it on the cheapest thing and watch it flail at a genuine reasoning problem that needed the heavy engine. Both are the same error: a fixed setting against a variable workload. The operator's move is to let the *cheap, routed* default carry the everyday work, and spend the expensive engine only on the turns that actually change the outcome.

Before Cursor's own vocabulary for this - pools, Auto, MAX Mode - price the mistake generically. In Cursor's terms the light corner is Auto routing from the included pool; the capable corner is a pinned frontier model billing per-token:

What makes Cursor different from the single-vendor agents - and the reason this chapter exists at all - is that "the model" isn't one company's model. Cursor lets you pick across vendors per chat, route automatically when you don't care, and run its own in-house agentic model. And underneath all of it sits a billing structure with two separate pools (laid out in [Pricing pools](/course/cursor/models/pricing-pools/)) that decides what a given request actually costs. Get the pools straight and the rest of the chapter is just judgment.

## The four moves of this chapter

- **The per-chat model picker and Auto** - the roster spans vendors, the picker is per-conversation, and Auto routes the everyday work from the discounted pool - [The per-chat model picker and Auto](/course/cursor/models/picker-and-auto/)
- **Composer and the two pools** - Anysphere's own agentic model, the discounted Auto + Composer pool versus per-token API, plus the MAX Mode button that moves a request between them (sometimes without asking) - [The two pools: discounted, per-token, and MAX Mode](/course/cursor/models/pricing-pools/)
- **Mixing models** - plan with a frontier model, build with a cheap one, and spend the expensive pool on the single turn that earned it - [Mixing models: plan with one, build with another](/course/cursor/models/mixing-models/)

The arc runs from the dial to the meter and back to judgment. The first lesson gives you the control surface and the default that should carry most of your week. The second names the one in-house model worth reaching for by name. The third turns the whole thing over and shows the billing structure underneath - because once you can see the pools, "which model?" stops being a taste question and becomes a spending one. The fourth puts both dials in the same loop: a strong engine on the design, a cheap one on the typing. The person who's merely *installed* Cursor pins a favourite model and pays frontier rates to stamp out boilerplate. By the end of this chapter you'll be the other kind of user - the one who lets Auto carry the everyday and reaches for the expensive engine on purpose.
