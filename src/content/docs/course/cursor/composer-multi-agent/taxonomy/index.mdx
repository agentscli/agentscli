---
title: "The three-flavor taxonomy"
description: "Local fan-out isolates files, subagents isolate context, Cloud Agents isolate the machine. Same instinct at three radii - pick the one the job wants."
---

Cursor's three flavors of more-than-one-agent, side by side. They are not interchangeable - they trade along different axes:

| | **Local fan-out** (worktrees) | **Subagents** | **Cloud Agents** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Where it runs** | Your machine, parallel worktrees | Your machine, isolated context | Remote cloud VM (Cursor-hosted or self-hosted machines) |
| **How you reach it** | Multi-agent fan-out, one prompt | Auto-delegate, `/name`, or a natural-language mention | Desktop, web, iOS; Slack, GitHub, Bitbucket, Linear, and @Cursor in Teams ([Teams integration](https://cursor.com/docs/integrations/microsoft-teams)); the API (launch channels as of September 2026: [Cloud Agents documentation](https://cursor.com/docs/cloud-agent), [subagents documentation](https://cursor.com/docs/subagents)) |
| **Shape** | One-shot, broadcast-and-compare | Delegated, defined ahead of time | Detached, fire-and-review |
| **Isolates** | The **filesystem** (worktrees) | The **context window** | The **whole machine** |
| **Best for** | Competing approaches or parallel slices you'll review together | A scoped, noisy, or repeatable job you don't want in your main thread | Work that shouldn't tie up your laptop and doesn't need your eyes mid-flight |

Read it as one question asked three ways - *where should this work happen, and how isolated does it need to be?* The local fan-out isolates **files** so a fleet of agents can edit at once without corrupting each other. A subagent isolates **context** so a noisy job doesn't bury your main thread. A Cloud Agent isolates the **whole machine** so the work leaves your laptop entirely. Same instinct - push work somewhere isolated and review what comes back - at three different radii. (The channel lists above are as of September 2026; the [Cloud Agents documentation](https://cursor.com/docs/cloud-agent) owns the current set.)

So before any heavy or parallel task, ask which radius it wants: fan it out locally to compare, delegate it to a subagent to keep your thread clean, or send it to the cloud to get it off your machine. Pick the wrong radius and you'll feel it - a Cloud Agent for a thirty-second scan is overkill, a local fan-out for a job with one obvious approach is just spending compute to feel busy.

:::note[A note on Composer-the-model]
"Composer" names two different things in Cursor, and this chapter is deliberately about only one of them. The **multi-agent surface** and its parallel worktrees are the subject here. **Composer the *model*** - Anysphere's own agentic coding model - is a model-roster concern, and it's covered in the [models chapter](/course/cursor/models/) alongside the rest of the picker and Auto routing. They share a name and shipped in the same release; they are not the same feature, and the model details live there to avoid duplicating them here.
:::

Parallelism settles how many agents work and where they run. It changes nothing about what any single one of them can *reach* - and note the machine boundary: a Cloud Agent reaches your repository in its cloud environment, not your laptop's local services, while a local agent reaches exactly your machine. What all of them reach for is the live systems, the repeatable procedures, and the rules that have to hold whatever the model decides - MCP, skills, commands, and hooks in the next chapter's terms. Those all live outside the repo, and each needs a different surface. [Next: extending Cursor with MCP, skills, commands and hooks](/course/cursor/extending/).
